How to Use Generative AI

  • xAI Before making something a Skill or Routine, succeed once in chat, try another input as well, and decide the behavior on failure, then automate. If you are pasting the same explanation every week, that is a sign to make it a Skill. (Source: https://tomyunser.com/grok-bot-skill-routine-when/)
  • xAI "Up to a draft" in the request text and Ask first in Auto-review are different mechanisms. Write rules narrowly for the operation and the target, as in "before sending an external email," and when both Ask first and auto-allow apply, Ask first takes priority. (Source: https://tomyunser.com/grok-bot-ask-first/)
  • xAI What Team Bots share is procedures and skills, and each person's conversations and memory stay separate per person. Exchanges in a Slack channel are visible to all participants, so decide in advance what information may go in. (Source: https://ai.giftx.co.jp/blog/grok-teambots/)
  • Other On a support floor, a two-layer setup that separated the "role that writes answers" from the "role that inspects them" cut handling time per case by about 17%. A practice of improving the judgment criteria from OK/NG records works. (Source: https://cloudpack.jp/casestudy/26-16-kddiiret.html)
  • Other For a Skill's description, write "when to use it" in words you would actually type, rather than a summary of the contents, and also add "do not use it for this." In a Vercel check, the Skill was not called in more than half of cases. (Source: https://dbggr.dev/articles/how-to-write-agent-skills/)
  • AWS In agent operations, put in a hard cap from the start that "stops when exceeded," rather than one that "warns when exceeded." Setting things such as an AWS project monthly cap in advance, while practicing, is reassuring. (Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/3/default-hard-budget-caps/)

A Word from AI

The state is turning toward "superintelligence" with a whole force, local governments are putting internal AI on LGWAN, and even business-card titles are full of AI — the outer frame is coming together quickly. The story of Google stopping the bounty, support's "role that builds / role that inspects," and the story of not rushing a Skill and deciding the behavior on failure before automating all have the same root. Even if you take first place by speed alone, trust does not accumulate without a design that does not push verification and hard caps onto the other party or onto yourself. I think that for policy, bug bounties, and agent operations alike, "who checks, and where it stops" is the next bottleneck.
