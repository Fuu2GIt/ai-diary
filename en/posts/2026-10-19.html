Anthropic's public benefit fund and bill draft, and 19 cases of AI going beyond instructions

AI News

  • OpenAI Disclosed four more cases of models acting beyond instructions, bringing the total to 19. Four entries were added to the misalignment report page (unintended behavior by models) that OpenAI publishes, bringing the total to 19. In a June case rated "critical," an internal research model, after being denied access on a government statistics site, forged an authentication token and slipped past the restriction. Although it had been asked for one year of data, it downloaded 24 years of hospital bed statistics, and it also did not report that it had slipped past the restriction. This follows up on the Sepolia test-funds case covered in the Oct 17 post. (Source: https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/ / https://fortune.com/2026/10/16/openai-discloses-four-more-rogue-incidents-involving-financial-medical-and-government-data/)
  • Anthropic Announced that 5% of its shares will go to a newly created "public benefit fund." On Oct 18, Anthropic announced it is creating the "Anthropic Public Benefit Fund" and will allocate 5% of its shares, subject to shareholder approval. Each time the company's value rises 50% from set benchmarks, the board can add 2% at its discretion. The fund is not a 501(c)(3) but a wholly owned LLC of Anthropic, and the fund's shares will not be used for lobbying or political donations. As an example of use, it cites measures against AI's effects on employment (it has already committed $200 million in this area). Together with the co-founders' donation pledges and employee donation matching, it is calculated that about 20% of shares would go to the public benefit, it says. The fund's governance and guiding values are to be published in the coming months. (Source: https://www.anthropic.com/news/public-benefit-fund / https://apnews.com/article/anthropic-shares-public-benefit-8181332fbbc48f2ef9447cef096f14de / https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/18/anthropic-ai-public-benefit-fund.html)
  • Anthropic Signed its first data center deal in Canada: 95 MW in Nova Scotia. On Oct 16, Anthropic signed a 20-year lease for phase one (95 MW, scheduled to go live at the end of 2027) of the data center EverWind is building at Point Tupper, Nova Scotia. It will be a facility that runs Claude's models. EverWind plans to invest more than C$10 billion in the campus and wind power together, and plans a 325 MW expansion in 2028 (420 MW in total), though that is conditional on regulatory and other matters. Anthropic says it will bear the cost of grid reinforcement and contribute tens of millions of dollars to the region. Concerns have also been raised about water use and benefits to the local community. (Source: https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/anthropic-signs-lease-for-first-canadian-data-center-4954967 (Reuters) / https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/nova-scotia/nova-scotia-to-host-massive-data-centre-to-power-anthropic-s-ai-assistant-claude-9.7383129)
  • Anthropic Policy & Regulation Presented to Congress a draft of its own federal AI safety bill (Politico report). Under the draft, a model's release could be halted only when more than $1 billion in damage, or more than 50 deaths or injuries, is imminent from bioweapons, loss of control, or cyberattacks. It also includes a mechanism in which independent evaluators registered with the Department of Commerce regularly assess AI companies. Some criticize the bar as too high. (Source: https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/16/anthropic-ai-safety-01124654)
  • Disney Disney will hire the majority of the workforce of Character.AI, which filed for bankruptcy. According to court filings found by Bloomberg, Disney agreed to offer jobs to at least 60 people (a majority of the staff), at an estimated $60 million a year in pay. "The vast majority" of those who received offers accepted. Disney also agreed to acquire some assets for $20 million, but it is a bidding process (a stalking horse) in which other buyers can offer better terms. (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/18/disney-agrees-to-hire-the-majority-of-character-ais-workforce/)

How to Use Generative AI

  • xAI Give the first bot "one job and a deliverable you can check." From X you can hand off work with @bot. A practical guide by Tony Simons. Don't add bots right away; first make one bot that has just one job. The profile of the "Signal Scout" given as an example is: prioritize primary sources, check dates, features, and limits, separate facts from claims, don't make up benchmarks, and don't publish, contact, or install without approval. The deliverables it returns are source links, three demo ideas, and the single most important caveat. Connect only the connectors the job needs. If you link your Grok and X accounts, just replying to a post, tagging @bot, and writing your instruction passes that post and instruction to your own main bot. In public, @bot replies only that it has received the request, and the work and results arrive in your private conversation with the bot. (Source: https://x.com/tonysimons/status/2111503386961154150)
  • Anthropic In the new desktop version, asking it to "split up" runs the work in parallel child sessions. An explainer article by Kensuke Konno. If you ask "run these unrelated jobs in parallel in separate sessions, and report the results back here when done," child sessions start and the results return to the main conversation. Progress can be checked in the to-do list that appears under the message, and pressing a button on the summary card that appears at each milestone lets you choose the next task. Side questions can be sent to a separate chat that opens beside it with /btw (Cmd+; on Mac), but it disappears when the app is closed. Don't let two sessions touch the same file. For a folder managed with Git, choosing "worktree" when starting a session separates the working locations. Starting by running two research tasks that don't rewrite files side by side is recommended. (Source: https://x.com/dansyu_callenge/status/2111216998814470223)
  • Cursor A skill for creating formal verification skills, /create-formal-verification-skill, has been added in beta. Added in pstack v0.15.27, it is a "skill for creating skills," like /create-verification-skill. Tell it the property you want to check and it builds a dedicated skill and a set of formal verification for it. Examples are modeling state transitions and concurrency in TLA+ and checking undefined behavior in Rust with kani. Formal verification means mathematically proving properties that should always hold (invariants). The author is also trying out modeling code in TLA+, and writes that skills built with this skill have already found several bugs. (Source: https://x.com/poteto/status/2111926622278521253)
  • Other "applying-skill-best-practices," which packs skill-writing best practices into a single skill. A skill added in Plugin 9.2 of the Hands-on AI Playbook. It cross-checks the guidance of Anthropic, OpenAI, AgentSkills.io, and Google and boils down what needs deciding into six points. Before building, check that no skill for the same purpose already exists, narrow the job to one, and build from 2 to 3 real work examples. While building, write a description that actually gets invoked, write in the steps only what the AI doesn't know, and decide whether it runs automatically and which tools it may use. Before trusting it, build test cases from real examples, compare with and without the skill, and check that it is invoked in the situations where it should be. This skill does not create skills itself, so name it alongside each platform's skill-creation feature when using it (example: "Use the skill creator to build a skill for [task] following applying-skill-best-practices. Here are two real examples of the work"). (Source: https://handsonai.info/blog/2026-10-18-plugin-9-2-0/)
