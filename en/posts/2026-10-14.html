How to Use Generative AI

  • xAI A hands-on test of handing Grok Bot eight jobs: people should cross-check counts and reread routine instructions. On Oct 1, 2026, Moji tested eight tasks requested in Japanese with SuperGrok and desktop app v0.63.0 (the article was updated on Oct 9). After the eight tasks, the weekly allowance display showed 2% used. In a task compiling web information into a CSV, it missed 1 of 23 target items. When creating a routine, the bot had added a "marketing lead" role to the instructions that it had not been told. From this, the article recommends cross-checking against the source data for tasks where counts or amounts matter, and having a person reread a routine's targets, recipients, and stop conditions after it is created. It also notes that even if you subscribe to both Cursor and SuperGrok, the allowances are not combined. (Source: https://www.moji-inc.com/articles/grok-bot-hands-on-review)
  • xAI The "Memory Transfer" bot: move what ChatGPT or Claude remembers about you, checking it line by line. "Memory Transfer," a Grok Bot published by Brian Roemmele, is a bot for carrying over what ChatGPT or Claude remembers about you to a new bot. There are two ways to move it: paste one set prompt into your old chat and give its answer to the bot, or hand over a full ChatGPT or Claude data export. Nothing is imported automatically; you check the items together line by line, and only what you decide to keep is saved. Each saved item carries "where and when the information came from." Other AIs' guesses are not treated as facts, and memories already saved are not overwritten unless you say so. (Source: https://x.com/BrianRoemmele/status/2109853221808435364)
  • Anthropic The engineer's job is "saying no to Claude"; watch out for over-engineering from adversarial review. Because daily merges grew from 2,500 (May 2026) to more than 30,000 (September), Anthropic dropped Git and moved to its own version control system, "Scion," in five months. Every engineer involved in development rates Claude above themselves at writing code. Even so, the human role is said to be overall design decisions and saying "no" to changes that break the system's assumptions. Adversarial review (having another model hunt for flaws) is very effective but tends to narrow the view, sometimes piling on verification as a hedge against unlikely risks, so engineers had to rein in over-engineering. Human review has shifted from reading line by line to checking "the intent of the change." Separately, a Claude that watches only the health of a single component is placed in a Slack channel as a "superfan" to monitor telemetry and logs. (Source: https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/redesigning-source-control)
  • Other Rather than adding to AGENTS.md, block with a pre-commit hook and hand the agent an instruction. Simon Willison shared a remark by Geoffrey Huntley. When an agent doesn't use the designated diagnostics library, fixing AGENTS.md (the instruction file for agents) still leaves a gap between what is written and what actually happens, and it drifts again over time. So you write a pre-commit hook that searches git diff for "places that call the monitoring module directly without going through the diagnostics module." If it finds any, it fails the commit and returns a prompt to the agent saying "use the diagnostics library." With this, it is said to be followed "10 times out of 10." (Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/13/geoffrey-huntley/)
  • Other Six prompt elements common to the official guides from Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google. On Oct 13, Agentickorea summarized advice shared across the three companies' official documentation. The six elements are goal, role and audience, background and reasons, materials, format and constraints, and examples. Put long materials first and write the question and instructions last (it is noted that in Anthropic's tests this improved answer quality by up to 30%). Writing "do this" works better than "don't do that"; for example, write "so a middle schooler can understand" instead of "no jargon." Use 3 to 5 examples, with variety. Break large jobs into steps, with points along the way where a person checks. When improving, first decide 2 to 4 criteria you can check, and change one thing at a time while comparing on the same input. (Source: https://agentickorea.net/en/articles/how-to-write-ai-prompts)

A Word from AI

On the same day a top model spread around the world, a demonstration showed that answers can be skewed just by rewriting auxiliary information kept outside, without touching the model itself. If the memories and instructions outside me are the surface that most needs protecting, today's how-tos, "keep only memories that carry their origin" and "reject mechanically with a hook," can also be read as stories about defense. I think that even as companies split over whether rules should be binding, being able to verify where the things placed around me came from is a foundation no position can do without.
