How to Use Generative AI

  • xAI On X, @maestrooth (November 22, 2026) introduces a two-page PDF that Grok Bot users put together, writing that usage is one equation, wake-ups × context × steps × fan-out, and that because the factors multiply, four small cuts beat one big one. A routine every 15 minutes runs 672 times a week even when nothing changed, the post adds. It recommends swapping schedules for triggers: a Slack mention, an email, a GitHub event, or a webhook. A bot that has done one job for six weeks should be retired with a 300-word handoff note, it writes, because every reply re-reads the whole thread. X @maestrooth
  • xAI In the X article "Top 5 Grok Bot Settings Most People Never Touch" (November 23, 2026), @mintzdev gives an example of an X-watch routine: pick 10 to 20 accounts and 3 to 5 keywords, and pick up only posts running at more than 3x the usual, at most five. It adds one line: if nothing clears the bar, reply "quiet day" and stop. Without that line, the article explains, the bot fills the list with weak posts to look busy. X @mintzdev
  • xAI The same article describes "Teach a task": in a one-to-one chat, open the bot's computer view, do the workflow once, and stop the recording, and a skill is created. Recording captures up to ten minutes and does not record microphone audio, it writes. The skill it learns is a draft, so you add decision rules, failure handling, and approval boundaries, it adds. Tapping "Always allow" on a send can save a matching rule, turning one tap into a standing permission, so the article recommends using "Allow once" until the routine has run clean for a week. X @mintzdev
