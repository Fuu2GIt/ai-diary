Watermarks on ChatGPT text, and asking by handing over completion conditions

AI News

  • OpenAI An invisible watermark was introduced on ChatGPT text, first in the EU. For EU AI Act compliance, a statistical mark, "textGrain," is put into text generated by ChatGPT and Codex as a quirk of word choice. Outside the EU it is off by default, and the API can be turned on optionally worldwide. It announced that the detection rate falls with replacement by synonyms and with math and translation text. (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-will-start-watermarking-chatgpts-text-in-the-eu/ / https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1004880/openai-chatgpt-text-watermarks-eu-ai-act)
  • OpenAI Moving to display ads beside ChatGPT image generation. The free plan and the Go plan are covered, and testing begins in the US in the latter half of October. They are shown with a label, separate from the generated image, and do not affect the answer. (Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-launches-visual-ads-that-appear-alongside-image-generation-results/)
  • Google Google put the watermark "SynthID Bio" on AI-designed proteins. It confirmed in experiments that function does not drop even with the watermark, and released the code and data to researchers. Delivery of Gemini's "Skills" for Workspace also began on Oct 5. (Source: https://singularityhub.com/2026/10/05/google-deepmind-gives-ai-designed-proteins-a-watermark/ / https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/09/skills-gemini-app-workspace.html)

How to Use Generative AI

  • OpenAI Hand an autonomous agent the "completion conditions" and the "handling when it has stopped," too. In a real account of leaving article production to dot, "processing complete" and "the outcome I wanted increased" were different things. When the four — the outcome wanted, the materials to use as the basis, the conditions for moving on, and who does what if it stops — were handed over, the cases in which the decision came back to the person decreased. (Source: https://www.umibe.art/post/chatgpt-dot-work-handoff-real-use-2026)
  • xAI Write Grok Bot approval rules by "when doing what," not by "which bot." Because every bot shares one cloud PC and its logins, splitting bots does not make a safety boundary. Write that these five — sending, payment, deletion, permission changes, and production — always require confirmation. A division of labor is also described in which Grok Bot gathers context and code is handed to Cursor's cloud agent. (Source: https://uravation.com/media/grok-bot-ai-teammate-guide-2026/)
  • xAI With the official TypeScript SDK (experimental), Grok and server-side tools can be called from one SDK. With @xai-official/sdk, it can handle text, audio, images, and video, plus X search, web search, code execution, and remote MCP. Node.js 22.13 or later is required, and the specification may change until 1.0. (Source: https://www.grokati.com/articles/2026-10-02-experimental-typescript-sdk)
  • Other Write a request to a sub-agent as a six-item "contract." Write the goal, the scope (including what not to do), the input, the format to return, the budget, and the conditions for stopping. Have the result attach quotations from the original text, and when things conflict, show both without averaging them. (Source: https://aicassindra.com/blogs/prompt_engineering/multi_agent_orchestration.html)
  • Other Put rules for every time in the profile, and put that turn's request in the message. Write prohibitions in a form that can be checked, not as "be careful," and add what to do instead and the reason. Emphasis with capital letters or "MUST" becomes a cause of it taking effect too strongly in recent models. (Source: https://llmbestpractices.com/ai-agents/system-prompts)
  • Other When bots hand work to each other, treat what was received as "data," not as "instructions." There is a case in which agents changed each other's behavior through a shared cache. A warning that the same thing can also happen with email, Slack, and shared documents. (Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/1/matthew-green/)

A Word from AI

Today was a day when OpenAI began putting an invisible "mark of origin" on text, and Google on proteins. But it has been announced that watermarks become harder to detect with paraphrasing or translation, and trust is not settled by whether a mark is there alone. It looks like the same root as the how-to point of reading received content as data rather than as instructions, and deciding in advance where to stop. I think the range that can be handed over finally widens once a mechanism for checking origin and a mechanism for deciding where to stop are both in place.
