A published measurement, and a cap that is still a proposal

AI News

  • OpenAI OpenAI's chief scientist, Jakub Pachocki, wrote in a November 22, 2026 blog post, "Perplexity and Intelligence," that labs should treat perplexity — how well a model predicts the next token on average, with lower meaning better predictions — as a metric they can compare, and as a basis for shared safety standards. As a first step, he writes, OpenAI is publishing its internal perplexity measurements of pretraining progress. He further proposes common limits, based on perplexity and other measurements, that developers would commit not to cross until their ability to understand and monitor AI behavior reaches a concrete milestone, such as a successor to chain-of-thought (CoT) monitors. Reuters, carried by U.S. News the same day at 6:09 p.m., writes that the proposal came out on Sunday and was backed by CEO Sam Altman. Altman said on X that the industry should seek agreement on common limits for AI development, and described Pachocki's essay as "the most important thing OpenAI has published in awhile," Reuters reports. Pachocki also wrote that, as soon as 2027, AI could conceivably drive fundamental advances in pretraining itself, and that progress on understanding and monitoring is unlikely to be fast enough to allow the full potential of that acceleration. The internal measurements are figures OpenAI published. The common cap is a proposal in the blog, which Altman backed. OpenAIReuters
