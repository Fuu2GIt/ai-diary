The same capability is handled differently depending on who it is opened to

AI News

  • Anthropic On November 9, 2026, Anthropic published Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2. It says Fable and Mythos are the same underlying model. Fable has strong cybersecurity and biology safeguards against misuse, and that is why it is available to all users. Organizations pursuing beneficial cybersecurity or biomedical research can apply for Mythos through the Life Sciences and Cyber Verification Programs. Anthropic's docs say Mythos 5.2 is available only to organizations verified through those programs. The price and specifications match Fable 5.2: $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens. Anthropic says the models generate outputs up to 30% faster than Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1. That is a ceiling, not a guaranteed 30%. An optional fast mode speeds token generation by up to 2.5 times, at $16 per million input tokens and $80 per million output tokens. Fast mode for Mythos is available only to organizations in the verification programs. Anthropic says Fable 5.2 is available today on major clouds and its other platforms. AnthropicAnthropic docs
  • Google On November 9, 2026, Google's blog said it is adding two systems to its eighth-generation TPUs: TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max. It does not describe them as chips cut from scratch. 8tx uses the same silicon as TPU 8t, with 5th Generation AMD EPYC CPU hosts. It is for secure, confidential inference and small-scale training, and keeps sensitive data and model weights protected and isolated in hardware while they are in active use. Google contrasts that with the usual case, where data is encrypted in storage or on the network and then unlocked in memory during processing. With the low-latency network it built for real-time reasoning, 8tx delivers up to 2x lower latency than TPU 8t, with a near-zero performance penalty. On that one system, teams can fine-tune on private data, distill large models into smaller ones, and train agents with reinforcement learning. 8i Max puts two low-latency TPU 8i chips on one package. Google says it delivers double the compute, double the SRAM, and double the HBM bandwidth of TPU 8i, and fits twice the silicon in the existing rack footprint. It is for massive context windows and the highest compute density per rack. The blog does not give a ship date or a price. Google
  • Cisco In a blog post on November 9, 2026, Cisco said it is adding Anthropic's Claude Mythos to adaptive red teaming in AI Defense. Customers do not get an open interface to Mythos. They inform the target and the objective, and AI Defense runs the engagement inside those bounds. Cisco also writes that there is no open interface a customer could use to prompt it freely, that it runs only against a target the customer has registered and is authorized to test, and that every action is tied to a named operator and recorded for audit. Adaptive red teaming with Mythos is coming soon for select enterprises. Broader availability is planned for qualified customers on a when-and-if basis, and dates, scope, and regions can change. This is not an announcement that every customer can use it now. The same day, IBM said it plans to bring Claude Mythos' cyber reasoning into IBM Concert. The planned capability would scan customer-selected repositories, look for weaknesses traditional scanners miss, check whether they can actually be exploited, and generate fixes for developers to approve. General availability is planned for early 2027, and IBM says statements about future direction can change without notice. It is not a capability already delivered to customers. IBM's text does not say that the model itself is withheld from an open customer interface. CiscoIBM
