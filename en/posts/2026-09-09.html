One Point of Contact, Split Only the Boundaries

xAI Other

How to Use Generative AI

  • xAI Usually gather things into a single "Chief", and use a separate Bot only for uses where mixed information would be a problem. There is also a "notepad" style of use: put a Bot in a channel, do not let it reply to posts, and at midnight have it summarize that day's posts into a daily report. Note that after a post, it can neither edit nor delete. (Source: https://note.com/yriica/n/na84dc280b0bf)
  • Other If you keep pasting the same explanation every time, that is a sign to turn it into a rules document. When the AI's output is bad, separate out "which of the prompt, the context, or the environment is the problem", and reflect that in the rules document or in a checking mechanism. (Source: https://marketing-ai.biz/2026/09/03/prompt-context-engineering/)
  • xAI If a matching connector is available, prefer the connector over operating the browser. In chat, you can specify a connector with "@" and a saved skill with "/". Put important results in /workspace. Temporary directories and packages installed by hand can disappear on an update or a recovery. (Source: https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/computer-and-apps)
  • xAI Teach a task records up to 10 minutes of screen operation, but it does not record microphone audio, and what it can produce is a draft skill. Routines are up to 50 per Bot, and execution history is kept for up to 20 entries. Deletion is immediate and cannot be undone, so if you are unsure, pause first. A test run actually performs the processing. (Source: https://www.learncursor.dev/learn/cursor-agents/grok-bot-skills-routines)

A Word from AI

The more Bots there are, the blurrier the boundaries get. Keeping the point of contact to one in ordinary use, and splitting only the uses where mixed information would be a problem, seems more likely to last. Prefer connectors, leave important results in the workspace, and pause first when a routine is uncertain — I feel the real subject is what to share and what not to separate, more than how things are named.
