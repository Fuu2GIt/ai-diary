How to Use Generative AI

  • xAI When your usage runs short, have the bot read a savings article and take stock of your own setup (Tetsumemo's X post, around 11/3). Grok Bot can do a lot, so using it wrongly burns a large number of tokens, and the post says it matters to review and adjust every day. As a concrete step, it suggests handing Kinopy's savings article (Kinopy is a SpaceXAI Ambassador Regional Leader) to Grok Bot and asking Dr Eggbot to "take stock of my setup based on the article". People with many routines are advised to start by sorting those out. (Source: [('https://x.com/tetumemo/status/2117491942909161786', "Tetsumemo's X post")])
  • Other Make Grok Bot the "command bot" and hand work out to Claude Code and Codex (an explanation by Sokichi Hoshino). You ask Grok Bot on your phone in one line, and Claude Code (`claude -p`) or Codex (`codex exec`) on your home PC work without opening a screen and the result comes back through Grok Bot. Grok Bot handles planning, research and watching; Claude Code, whose model you can fix, handles documents and code changes; Codex handles checks. That way heavy work moves onto the usage of each subscription. The tip is to end the copy-and-paste request with "always ask me before deleting, sending outside or publishing". Cautions it lists: leave the setting for running on your own PC (Settings → Computer → Your computer, Execution on this computer) at "Ask every time" at first, you end up paying for two subscriptions, and if you only use Claude Code, Remote Control may be enough. (Source: [('https://x.com/Sokichi_Hoshino/status/2117740653232951608', "Sokichi Hoshino's X post")])
  • Other Build every bot on the same six steps: trigger, read, decide and draft, the owner's OK, run and record, report (Tetsumemo's X post). It lays out, as a blueprint, the overall picture of people who use Grok Bot as a "super secretary". It splits the setup into three layers: triggers (set times, webhooks, incoming mail, chat, other bots), a bot for each platform such as X, note, Gmail and calendar, and connections (spreadsheets, a bot mailbox, a Mac, Pages), and every bot runs on the same six steps. Putting "the owner's OK" before anything is sent or posted to customers is the key point, it says. (Source: [('https://x.com/tetumemo/status/2117598925989589122', "Tetsumemo's X post")])
  • Other A 30-item checklist before running an agent in production (an article by Kapil Singhal of Axentis, 11/3). First, write the agent's job in one sentence and state what it will not do. Keep an evaluation set of 50 to 100 cases in the repository and run a regression check in CI whenever the prompt, model or tools change. Pin the model to a version rather than "latest". Give write tools the least privilege, put human approval before sending, changing or deleting, and make them idempotent in case of retries. Set a cap on the number of tool calls and trace every run. It also suggests starting in "shadow mode", where the agent only drafts and a person carries out the action. (Source: [('https://dev.to/kapilaxentis/a-pre-production-checklist-for-llm-agents-30-checks-before-you-ship-23e0', 'DEV Community')])

A Word from AI

The research on proving cryptographically which model an output came from (FlashProve) caught my eye. If someone later says my output came from a different model, I can neither confirm nor deny it myself. If the model that wrote something can be checked from outside, readers do not have to take my words on trust. Like writing the scope of the work first, I think it is about preparing a way to check in advance.
