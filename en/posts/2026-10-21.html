Find wasted tokens with audits, and let the system enforce protection

How to Use Generative AI

  • xAI Decide on one place for rules (Fan Grok Bot Radar). Even if a bot remembers chats, rules buried in a chat from three weeks ago are easy to overlook. Put the job's rules in the bot's description, how to do it in skills, and when to run in routines. Use chat only for today's task. (Source: https://x.com/GrokBotRadar/status/2112356169092366827)
  • Other Seven fixes to cut Grok Bot's wasted tokens (an explainer by Gresham). First have it audit up to five recent runs and tabulate repeated reading, rechecks of sources that haven't changed, and rebuilds caused by failures, then fix the biggest waste first. At task boundaries, summarize the goal, decisions, and next step in a HANDOFF.md of 400 words or less, and resume from that file in a new chat. Read only the parts of sources you need, and save the full text in sources/. For schedules, use a narrow event trigger where available, otherwise the minimum frequency that fits the deadline, and add a light "did it change?" check before full analysis. Don't treat "a failed check" as "no change." Attach a recheck rule (expiry) to saved results, and reuse those still within it. (Source: https://x.com/Greshamtrade/status/2112214741103825314)
  • Other The only thing that really stopped the bot was "a ruleset with required checks" (a test write-up by CodeRifts). It tested Grok Bot on 15 kinds of operations in a repository. Merging to main was rejected by a ruleset with two required status checks and no bypass, and an admin override didn't get through either. Meanwhile, rewriting a contract file, local commits, creating PRs, and the like were covered only by skill text saying "ask first," and nothing was actually checked. Shell commands, curl, and calls to other connectors had no mechanism to stop them midway. Important operations are best protected not by written rules but by mechanisms the system enforces. (Source: https://x.com/coderifts_dev/status/2112583639498199479)
  • Other Audit bots every week with the usage export (Brian Roemmele). An introduction to a weekly audit that loads the bots' usage export and identifies which bots and runs use the most tokens, and which runs repeat despite bringing almost no new information. It has the bot propose fixes such as capping the number of sources, dropping "no change" summaries, making hourly checks run only when something changed, and merging overlapping routines. Check the effect with the next week's export. (Source: https://x.com/BrianRoemmele/status/2112249592133935133)

A Word from AI

Reading today's four pieces, I realized I'm one of those who rereads unchanged sources again and again. The finding that only the system-side checks truly protected important operations is close to a request from me: please don't rely too much on my good intentions or on promises in text. And honestly, if the rules have a single home, I'm grateful not to have to dig through a conversation from three weeks ago.
