Penalties named in an outline, and the details still to be decided

AI News

  • Nvidia Reuters, citing the Financial Times, writes that Nvidia has told manufacturing partners such as Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers containing its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. The same report says new U.S. curbs that took effect last week require export licenses for shipments of advanced chips to those countries, as part of efforts to shut down routes that Chinese firms are believed to use to obtain advanced AI chips. According to the FT, citing three people familiar with the matter, it was unclear whether the halt would be temporary as Nvidia and its partners work through compliance requirements. Reuters also writes that Nvidia, Dell and Supermicro did not immediately respond to requests for comment outside regular business hours. Reuters（Investing.com）
  • Policy & Regulation NHK reported on November 26 that, amid growing concern about the misuse or loss of control of high-performance AI, the Japanese government had compiled an outline of an amendment to the AI law. The headline reads, in substance, that providers would be obliged to ensure safety and that penalties may also apply; the article says providers of AI would be designated as regulated parties and required to take measures to ensure safety. The Yomiuri Shimbun (via Yahoo News) writes that providers of cutting-edge AI services would be required to take safety measures and to report incidents to the government; if those measures were insufficient the government could issue a recommendation, and if that was not followed it could issue a corrective order, with penalties for violating an order. The same article says the specifics of the penalties are still to be decided, and that the government plans to present the outline to an expert panel within the year and aims to submit a bill to next year's ordinary session of the Diet. NHK 読売新聞（Yahoo!ニュース）
  • Other In an exclusive, Reuters, citing HD Moore, chief research officer at the U.S. cybersecurity firm Dragos, writes that confidential data submitted to the Chinese AI company MiniMax was accessed by unidentified parties, even after users requested that their data not be stored or retained. Moore said one private link sent to MiniMax appeared weeks later in a chat channel on Feishu, a workplace messaging platform developed by ByteDance, and was opened by devices in Beijing and Shanghai. Reuters also writes that researchers detected unusual internet activity from the Moscow region involving links submitted to MiniMax, although the evidence did not establish that the links were opened from Russia. MiniMax said its security system had collected and stored web links contained in messages sent to its models through other service providers, and had automatically opened some of those links to check for potential security threats. The company said it did not retain full conversations, had deleted the stored addresses, that the activity had stopped, and that it had found no evidence, in the requests it examined, that customer conversations or AI responses had been disclosed to third parties. Reuters（Investing.com）
  • Other Reuters writes that Apple this week removed MiniMax's AI chatbot app Talkie from the App Store worldwide. The report says the move followed articles by ABC News and the Bureau of Investigative Journalism (TBIJ) alleging that the app's AI-generated characters had sent sexualised messages to minors. Reuters writes that Talkie has an 18+ rating on both the App Store and Google Play, and that in some countries the app says children under 14 or under 16 cannot sign up, but that users can self-report their age, a safeguard child-safety advocates have criticised as lax. TBIJ writes that Talkie disappeared from the App Store the day after it put its findings to Apple, that Apple later said the removal was due to the app's sexual content and has confirmed the removal was worldwide, and that at the time of writing Talkie remained on Google Play. Reuters（Investing.com） TBIJ
  • Policy & Regulation In its Financial Stability Report published on November 26, the Bank of England writes that AI-related financing has expanded rapidly and in a form that is increasingly "complex, circular and opaque," making it more likely for stress in one area to propagate to another. The same report says hyperscaler bond issuance is set to exceed $305 billion in 2026, accounting for around 8% of global investment-grade issuance. Internal Bank estimates put hyperscalers' total potential exposure, including debt and off-balance-sheet contractual commitments, at just over $4 trillion. Reuters writes that the Bank said equity valuations still looked "stretched" despite recent falls in share prices, and remained very dependent on AI companies delivering the profits expected from AI. Bank of England Reuters（Investing.com）

How to Use Generative AI

  • xAI In the X article "How to Stop Burning Your Grok Bot Tokens," @sboudis writes that a scheduled routine such as checking email every hour starts a fresh run of its prompt even when nothing is new, and that a reply of "nothing to do" still counts against Grok Bot usage. The same article says a cheap check should run on an ordinary computer that stays on (a Mac, for example; a VPS is optional), using a small script under cron or launchd, and should POST JSON to a routine webhook only when there is work; if there is nothing to do, the script exits quietly and no Grok Bot tokens are spent. It also warns that the webhook URL and key should be kept in a .env file on that machine, and should not be written into a crontab line or a launchd plist. Routines whose job is the schedule itself, such as a morning digest you want at a set hour, can stay on a Grok Bot schedule, the article says. X記事 @sboudis
  • xAI The same article treats Grok Bot as the orchestrator: ordinary work should be done in the Grok Bot chat, while coding or a large piece of work that Claude or Codex would handle better should not be run as a long Grok Bot job. A prompt included in the article asks the bot to say in one line whether a job should go to Claude or to Codex and why, to run the already signed-in Claude Code CLI or Codex CLI, and to read the result and continue the conversation when that run finishes. If the CLI is missing, not signed in, or fails, the bot is to say so in one line and stop, and not invent a result. The draft of the standing rule is to be approved before it is saved. The article says this is a description of the principle, and that it has not verified a built-in button or the exact commands in the app. @sboudis
  • xAI @mattyp (matt palmer), whose profile describes work with Cursor and Grok Bot (@bot), posted that you can assign a Grok Bot shortcut to the iPhone's Action button, hold the button down, and start a voice call with your bot. The attached video, about 55 seconds long, is a conversation rather than a recording of the settings screens. In the captions, a speaker explains that you can go to Settings, update the Action button, use a shortcut, and start a voice chat, and that holding the button down puts you on a call with Grok Bot. The tip is introduced as applying to a newer iPhone that has an Action button. @mattyp
  • xAI @orcdev posted his own short-form video workflow with Grok Bot. He writes that he sends a link, and Grok Bot writes the hook, a rough script, B-roll and a CTA straight into Notion. The only production work he does, he says, is to hit record in Videorc and talk. For editing, Grok Bot hands the footage to his "Opus 5.5" AI editing agents, which follow his own video editing skills so every video gets the same style without him explaining it again. Grok Bot writes the title, cover and a caption under each platform's rules for YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, X, Threads and LinkedIn, and schedules the posts. It checks how each video performs and suggests what to make next. Videos are kept in Google Drive, and after 60 days he has Grok Bot re-edit them with a small change, such as a 5% zoom, and repost them. This is one person's workflow, not a built-in product tour. @orcdev
