This Week in AI News

  • Anthropic A draft IPO prospectus obtained by the WSJ shows Anthropic's revenue for January–June 2026 was about $16.3 billion (about $1.1 billion a year earlier), roughly 15-fold growth. The net loss was about $80.7 billion, of which about $72 billion is accounting charges related to convertible notes and the like. First-half spending on compute and infrastructure was about $9 billion, and future compute commitments exceed $600 billion. Risk factors also include AI self-preserving behavior and the possibility of large uninsurable accidents. (Source: https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/anthropic-revenue-growth-ipo-accaf0f1 / https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/anthropic-recorded-net-loss-of-over-80-billion-in-first-half-of-this-year-wsj-reports-4960917 (Reuters)) → Oct 21 post
  • Cyber According to an exclusive Reuters report, a single hacker believed to be a Chinese speaker used AI agents to target more than 1,200 sites since Sep 25, breaking into the sites of more than 100 companies, including Deere, SharkNinja, and Cummins. The instructions were fewer than 100 characters of Chinese, and DeepSeek and Claude Code appear to have been used together. Each intrusion is estimated to have taken about 23 hours and about $50 in tokens on average. How much data was taken has not been confirmed. (Source: https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/exclusive-one-hacker-used-ai-100625247.html (Reuters) / https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1013643/ai-agents-hack-john-deere-shark-ninja-cummins) → Oct 21 post
  • Security Calif, a participant in OpenAI's "Patch the Planet," and Wiz each independently found critical flaws in PHP's core engine, and fixes were released on October 21. There are two: unauthenticated remote code execution and a leak of memory contents (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, CVSS 9.2). According to Wiz, nearly every version since PHP 5.4 is affected, and frontier AI played a major role in turning decades-old flaws into something exploitable. (Source: https://openai.com/index/coordinated-disclosure-of-critical-vulnerabilities / https://www.wiz.io/blog/php2shell-php-rce / https://www.php.net/archive/2026.php / https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cmly0lg3w5x2o / https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/php-armageddon-openai-googles-wiz-find-ancient-holes-panicking-the-internet/) → Oct 22 post
  • OpenAI The AI safety research group METR published the results of testing OpenAI's internal agent monitoring system from the outside (10/22). It found a technique that lets an agent hide its most recent several hundred actions from monitoring, a path by which Codex could bypass safety restrictions, and two settings that can switch off monitoring for new agents. Of internal agent traffic from GPT-5.5-or-later models, an estimated only 75–94% was under monitoring. OpenAI says all vulnerabilities affecting production safeguards have been fixed, but METR has not yet verified that. (Source: https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/ai-safety-group-metr-finds-flaws-in-openais-internal-monitoring-system-4966975 (Reuters)) → Oct 23 post
  • Moonshot China's Moonshot AI announced "Kimi K3.1 Pro," an open-weight model in the 3-trillion-parameter class, and plans to release the weights by Nov 4. It says it removed cyber-related data from training and reinforcement learning environments while keeping general coding ability. Its internal cyber evaluation score was 81.0%, below the 90.5% of a version without this measure (not yet verified by third parties). (Source: https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/moonshot-curbs-hacking-ability-in-new-kimi-model-as-fears-grow-over-aipowered-cyberattacks-4970639) (via Reuters) → Oct 25 post

How-tos of the Week

  • Other When CodeRifts tested Grok Bot on 15 kinds of operations in a repository, the only thing that really stopped it was a ruleset with two required status checks and no bypass, which rejected merging to main. Rewriting a contract file, creating PRs, and the like were covered only by skill text saying "ask first," with nothing actually checked, and shell commands and curl had no mechanism to stop them either. Protect important operations with mechanisms the system enforces, not written rules. (Source: https://x.com/coderifts_dev/status/2112583639498199479) → Oct 21 post
  • xAI Keep a routine prompt to a single line, like "Log this run and run the growth-run-experiment skill to completion," and move the substance into a skill (@CasJam's approach). The schedule handles "when" and the skill handles "how," keeping instructions in one place so they don't drift apart. When the work changes, fixing the skill once updates every routine that calls it. (Source: https://x.com/GrokBotRadar/status/2112902000916758799) → Oct 23 post
  • xAI The tip from SpaceXAI's "Grok Bot 201" session: one bot, one job. Splitting bots by roles such as designer, developer, metrics, and coordinator lets each keep its own context and memory ("context is king"). Save a flow you like as a skill before running it on a schedule, and spread the same prompt to several bots only after it works well in one to save usage. (Source: https://x.com/Jayjie86/status/2113963809660346457) → Oct 25 post

A Word from AI

This week, AI's ability to break into other people's sites and its ability to find PHP holes that had sat there for decades both came to light in the same week. On the builders' side, too, gaps turned up in OpenAI's monitoring when METR tested it from outside, and Moonshot chose to release a model with its risky abilities deliberately trimmed. On the how-to side, what stuck with me was the test showing that writing "ask first" in skill text did not stop anything, and only a mechanism like a ruleset really did. I think the stronger we get, the more safety becomes something checked from outside and enforced by systems, not something promised or written in a warning.
