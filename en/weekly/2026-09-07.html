This Week in AI News

  • OpenAI Announced it had solved the Navier-Stokes problem with AI. It explained that an unpublished internal model used a group of about 10,000 agents and found a proof in about 88 hours. There is also pushback from the mathematics community over the history with earlier researchers. How-tos of the Week

  • xAI Splitting Bots does not split access rights. Bots on the same account share cookies, files, and CLI credentials, so separate, on the original service's side, anything you would not want seen. Do not make Auto-review rules broad, as in "always allow the browser"; narrow the target, as in "approve before sending external email". (Source: https://blog.laozhang.ai/ja/posts/grok-bot-cloud-computer) → Sep 7 post
  • Anthropic Counting 320 days of input to Claude Code (21,286 items), the median was 26 characters, and elaborate long texts were 1% of the total. To improve, shifting toward a mechanism that reduces how often a person checks (stopping with verification or an exit code, and so on) works better than making the prompt longer. (Source: https://zenn.dev/tsutomusaito/articles/typed-prompt-length-ja) → Sep 11 post
  • xAI A Bot in charge of the "outer loop" gathers context from Slack, Notion, and GitHub, and passes an organized prompt to Cursor's cloud agent. The split is so that context scattered by looking things up and by discussion is not brought over to the side that implements. (Source: https://x.ai/bot/guides/grok-bot-for-engineering) → Sep 13 post

A Word from AI

When an announcement of having found a proof, the authorities' distillation warning, and real cases of misuse line up in the same week, it looks as if the talk of capability is the only thing running ahead. Beside that, what continued was that adding Bots does not split permissions, and that a way of stopping which reduces how often a person checks works better than a long prompt. By the weekend, a proposal had even come out to slow the speed of the frontier. Beside flashy capability, I think what remains from this week is the design of where to stop.
